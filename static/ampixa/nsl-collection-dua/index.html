NSL data plan

What to record with one signer this week, why each piece matters, and what we can do with the NTV news data we are scraping while the signer records.

Why data, and not a bigger model

For 74% of test words, the reference pile has only one clip of the right word. Words with one clip score 28%; words with two or more score 43%. Fine-tuning (28%), a trained layer (29.7%), voting, centering and synthetic speed copies all failed to beat the frozen baseline. The one real gain, filmstrip K=2 at 39.3%, needed no training. The model is short of examples of each sign from different people.

The 112 hours of parliament video carry ASR captions of what the speaker said, while the interpreter signs the gist. That mismatch is why earlier sign-to-text runs collapsed to repeating माननीय सदस्यहरु. Clean, matched labels come first. The broadcast data becomes useful once a good word model can label it.

3b Continuous signing, news style

WhyThe NTV and parliament interpreters sign continuously about government, health, roads and elections. Our dictionary clips are slow citation signs about animals and food. The model has never seen a labelled example of news signing.
What we do with itUse these as the reference for news spotting, as the first labelled news-style test set, and to set the confidence threshold for mining the broadcast.
What it provesWhether our word model can find news words inside fluent signing, which decides whether mining the 112 h can work at all.

C. Annotate real broadcast: 30 minutes of NTV interpreter video

The signer watches short NTV segments (1–2 minutes each) and writes down every sign they recognise, with a rough time. Use the pose-only replay or a local player; the video stays on our machine.

  • This is the only way to get labelled real broadcast signing. Everything else is a stand-in.
  • 30 minutes of fluent signing is roughly several hundred signs. It becomes the ruler for every mining round.
  • Mark signs as sure or unsure. Note fingerspelled names.

5 NTV news data: what we do without the signer

The scrape keeps running in the background. None of these steps needs NSL knowledge.

  1. Extract pose, then delete the video. The models only use body and hand points, and the provenance rules say pose only.
  2. Drop non-lesson videos. In the timing check, 4 of 7 wrong spans came from one intro video. Filter by title.
  3. Timing check on news spans. The same 100-clip check, on the news interpreter. Anyone can tick it.
  4. Count words in captions. Which dictionary words appear in news captions, and how often. This list decides which words to film next.
  5. Mining run. The best model (filmstrip K=2) scans the news pose for strong words and keeps only very confident hits. These go in a queue for the signer to confirm next week. Only confirmed hits join the pile.

Captions are what the anchor says; the interpreter signs the gist. That is why training directly on caption text failed and collapsed to repeating one phrase. The news data becomes useful through confirmed spots, not through its captions.

5b What the 112 hours of parliament video need

The weak labels cannot be used as they are. These are the steps, in order, that turn the broadcast into training data.

  1. Pose extraction, then delete video. docs/DATA_PROVENANCE.md commits to keeping pose only. Downloads are running; about 431 videos (99 GB) were on disk at last check.
  2. A ruler. About 100 randomly picked spans per tier, checked by hand. 100 checks give the percent correct to about ±10 points; 400 give ±5. Pick at random, never the easy ones.
  3. Two separate checks. Timing (is a sign happening in this span?) can be ticked by anyone, using data/timing_review/review.html. Word identity (is it this word?) needs a signer.
  4. Do our strong words appear? Count how often the well-recognised words show up in the captions. A word that never appears in parliament cannot be spotted there.
  5. Spot, filter, check. Slide the word model over the video, keep only confident hits for strong words, then hand-check about 100 hits. Only if enough are right do those spots become training labels.

6 The smallest seed that can label the 112 h and NTV

No amount of recording labels every second of 112 hours by hand. The goal is a seed: enough clean data that the model can find words in the broadcast by itself, so a signer only has to confirm, not annotate. These sizes are estimates, set by what our measurements suggest; each mining round tells us whether they are enough.

Seed pieceSizeWhy that size
Broadcast vocabularyTop ~100 news words, each with clips from 2+ different signers (~200–300 clips)Words with 2+ clips score 52% vs 35% (filmstrip K=2). A word with one clip is hard to spot even in isolation.
Repeated phrases10 phrases × 3 takesPhrases repeat hundreds of times, so a few good references cover a lot of broadcast time.
Annotated broadcast~30 minutes, glossed with rough timesThe ruler: measures what fraction of confident spots are right, and sets the confidence cut-off.
Confirmations per round100–200 mined hits, about 1 signer-hourEach confirmed hit is a new real example from a new signer, which is exactly what the model lacks.

How it improves the model

  1. Round 0. Seed recorded. The model scans the 112 h + NTV pose for the seed words and phrases; only very confident hits are kept.
  2. Check. The signer confirms or rejects 100–200 hits. Measure precision against the annotated 30 minutes.
  3. Grow. Confirmed hits join the pile. The pile now holds broadcast-style examples, so the next scan finds more, including words signed fast or blended.
  4. Repeat. Each round adds examples from the broadcast itself. Coverage grows round by round; the 112 h become labelled a little at a time, never all at once.
  5. Then train. Once a few thousand confirmed spots exist, the broadcast becomes real continuous training data with labels that match the signing, unlike the caption text that made sign-to-text collapse.

Decide after each round

  • Precision of confident hits ≥ 70%: keep mining with this threshold.
  • 40–70%: raise the threshold or add references for the failing words before the next round.
  • Under 40%: stop mining. The word model is not ready; record more vocabulary first.

Track one number across rounds: minutes of broadcast with at least one confirmed sign. If it stops growing, the seed is too small.

Schedule

About 90 minutes with the signer per session. Short sessions keep signing natural.

Day 1

Consent form, signer ID. Pilot 10 words, 2 takes each. 49 letters, 3 takes each.

Day 2

15 news words already in the dictionary. 20 new news words.

Day 3

20 everyday sentences. 20 spelled names.

Day 4

10 news phrases × 3 takes. 20 news-style sentences at news speed.

Day 5

Annotate 30 minutes of NTV interpreter video. One hour: word check on the 100 YouTube spans.

Day 6 (no signer)

Pose extraction. Pilot re-run with the paired check. Webcam letter test. Mining round 0 on the broadcast pose, queue for next week's confirmations.

Decide before Day 5

  • Pilot words: keep filming if the filmed words improve with the paired check REAL; stop if they don't.
  • Letters: the demo is usable for real signers if webcam top-1 is close to 78%; if it falls far below, retrain with the new poses before anything else.

Rules for every recording

  • Consent first. Use the forms in docs/consent/. The recording page src/community_dashboard.html already exists.
  • Never mix test and training signers. Results are only honest if the test signer was never seen in training.
  • Different people beat more clips: 5 people × 2 clips is better than 1 person × 10.
